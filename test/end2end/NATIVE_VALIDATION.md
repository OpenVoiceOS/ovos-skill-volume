## How the golden test decides

For each locale, at least 80% of the golden rows must route to their labelled intent. Every intent with rows in the locale must route at least one of its rows. No negative row may route to this skill, except the measured false claims in `NEGATIVE_KNOWN_CLAIMS` in `test_golden_utterances.py`.

## What a native speaker checks

A row with `machine_generated: true` was written by a model. A row with `needs_manual: true` has no native check yet. For each such row, check two things:

1. The sentence is something a speaker of that language says to a voice assistant.
2. The sentence means what its `intent_label` does: `volume_level` sets a named level (high, low, maximum, medium, default), `volume_reset` returns to the default level, `volume_max_boost` turns the volume all the way up, `change_volume` sets a number, and `volume_mute_toggle` switches mute on or off.

When a row is correct, set `needs_manual` to `false`. When a row is wrong, rewrite the sentence and keep the old text in `rewritten_from`.

Rows with `rewritten_from` replace older rows that were copies of template lines. A golden row must never equal a line of the locale's `.intent` files after expansion, because the test then measures nothing. A row must also not be a line plus one or two words, or a line with one word changed.
